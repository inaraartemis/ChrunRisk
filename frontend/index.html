What the Model Tells Us

Key patterns discovered from 7,043 telecom customers, explained in plain English.

Driver #1

Contract Type

Customers on Month-to-Month contracts churn at nearly 3× the rate of annual subscribers. Without a lock-in, every billing cycle is a churn opportunity.

Offer annual upgrade incentives to at-risk customers.
Driver #2

Monthly Charges

High-cost plans — particularly Fiber Optic subscribers — show significantly elevated churn. Price sensitivity is one of the strongest behavioral signals.

Target high-spend customers with personalized bundle discounts.
Driver #3

Customer Tenure

The first 12 months are the most vulnerable window. Short-tenure customers have not yet formed a loyalty habit and are easiest to lose.

Launch a structured onboarding program for new customers.
Driver #4

Add-On Services

Subscribers without Online Security, Tech Support, or Device Protection churn more. Add-ons create stickiness and perceived value.

Cross-sell add-ons to customers with bare-bones plans.
Driver #5

Payment Method

Customers paying by Electronic Check show the highest churn rates — often indicative of a less committed, comparison-shopping mindset.

Promote auto-pay enrollment with a loyalty reward.
Driver #6

Internet Service Type

Fiber Optic users churn more than DSL users despite paying a premium — signalling unmet quality expectations relative to cost.

Conduct satisfaction surveys for fiber subscribers proactively.

About the Dataset

This model was trained on the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset, a widely used benchmark in the churn prediction domain.

7,043 Customer Records
19 Input Features
26.5% Churn Rate
84.2% Model ROC-AUC
SourceIBM Sample Data — Telco Customer Churn (publicly available via IBM Analytics)
Features IncludeDemographics (gender, senior citizen, partner, dependents), service usage (phone, internet, streaming, security add-ons), contract details (type, billing method, tenure), and charges (monthly & total).
Target VariableBinary — whether the customer left the company within the last month (Churn: Yes / No).
Model UsedRandom Forest Classifier wrapped in a Scikit-Learn pipeline with One-Hot Encoding and Standard Scaling. Baseline Logistic Regression also evaluated for comparison.
